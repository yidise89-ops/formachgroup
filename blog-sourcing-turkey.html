HOW TO SOURCE
INDUSTRIAL EQUIPMENT
FROM TURKEY

A practical guide for African buyers — from finding the right supplier to getting your machinery delivered safely and on time.

Introduction

TURKEY: A GLOBAL MANUFACTURING HUB

Turkey has emerged as one of the world's leading manufacturers of industrial machinery, construction equipment, and general industrial products. With over 80,000 manufacturing companies and a strong export culture, Turkey offers African buyers a compelling combination: European-standard quality at competitive prices, short lead times, and flexible minimum order quantities.

For buyers in Mali, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroun, Morocco, Algeria and across the African continent, Turkey represents a viable and increasingly popular alternative to Europe, China or the USA for sourcing industrial equipment.

Step 1 — Define Your Technical Specifications

Before approaching any supplier, prepare a clear technical specification for what you need. For machinery, this typically includes: capacity requirements, power supply (voltage and frequency), operating environment (ambient temperature, altitude, humidity), required certifications (CE, ISO), and any brand preferences or equivalents.

A precise specification protects you from receiving non-compliant equipment and allows you to compare quotes on an equal basis. Vague requests like "a 200 t/h crusher" will generate widely varying quotes that are difficult to compare.

Step 2 — Find the Right Supplier

Turkish suppliers can be found through trade directories (Kompass, TurkishExporter.net), trade shows (WIN Eurasia, BAUMA Turkey), or through procurement intermediaries based in Turkey. Not all Turkish manufacturers have strong English or French communication, so working with an intermediary who speaks your language and knows the local market is often valuable.

Key questions to ask a potential supplier: How long have they been manufacturing this product? Can they provide references from African customers? Are their products CE certified? Do they have export experience to your destination country?

Step 3 — Negotiate and Verify the Offer

When you receive a proforma invoice, verify: the technical specifications match your request exactly, the price is EXW (Ex-Works) or FOB clearly stated, payment terms are specified (typically 30-50% advance, balance before shipment), lead time is confirmed in writing, and warranty conditions are clear.

Always request factory visit images or a factory audit for large orders. For smaller orders, request quality control inspection reports and test certificates before shipment.

Step 4 — Manage Shipping and Customs

From Turkey, your equipment can be shipped by sea (most cost-effective for heavy machinery), air (for urgent small parts), or road (to North Africa). The main Turkish export ports are Mersin, Istanbul (Ambarlı), İzmir and Gemlik.

Ensure you receive all necessary export documentation: commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin (Turkey), bill of lading, CE declaration of conformity, and any product-specific certificates. Missing documents are the most common cause of customs delays in Africa.

Step 5 — Work with a Trusted Intermediary

For buyers who cannot travel to Turkey or do not have established supplier relationships, working with a Turkey-based procurement intermediary like Formach Group removes most of the risk. An intermediary handles supplier identification, technical verification, price negotiation, quality control and export logistics — allowing you to focus on your project.
